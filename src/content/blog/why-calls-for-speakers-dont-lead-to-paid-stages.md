For a while, a big part of our work at Brux was applying to calls for speakers for our clients. We sent a lot of applications, and very few of them ended in a paid keynote.

So we changed the approach. Now we skip the application and write to the event organizer directly, and it has worked much better for us. Every other week, I’ll share here what we learn from those conversations.

## Why going direct works better

The first reason is simple. When you apply to a call for speakers, your application goes through a form and a committee before anyone with a budget sees it. When you email the organizer, you’re talking to the person who makes the decision.

The second is the fee. Most open calls come with fixed terms, and many of them don’t pay at all. A conversation with the organizer gives you room to explain what you charge and why, and to negotiate.

The third is the one I care about most. Organizers rarely run just one event. Many of them put on several a year, so if they like working with you, they tend to book you again. One good reply can turn into several stages over time, and that almost never happens with a call for speakers.

## If you want to try it

Start with organizers in your niche whose audience matches your talk. Keep the first email short, say which talk fits their people and add a link to your demo reel. And don’t stop after one message, because a lot of replies come after a polite follow up.
